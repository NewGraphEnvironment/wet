## Findings

- **[severity: bug]** vignettes/short-record-flow.Rmd:116-121 (and the table's "Average" rows, 257-265) — The answer sentence says "Maxan Creek's average flow in 2011–2024 was 56% lower than in 1931–1980" (migration "14% lower"; min7 "about two thirds"). The number is the pilot's change, a difference of mean log departures: a ratio of geometric means of the yearly window means. Only the figure caption says so ("typical flow (the geometric mean)"). For skewed spawning flows the two differ a lot. On the bundled series, the arithmetic period means give spawning −43.6% (not −55.9%) and migration −9.4% (not −14.1%). Every 1981–2010 spawning departure averages well below 0 in log for the same reason. A reader of the opening, which the writing convention says must stand alone, takes "average flow ... was 56% lower" as the period average and gets a figure 12 points too large. Say "typical (geometric-mean) flow" in the answer and the table, or quote the quantity by its definition. The figure guard at 252-255 pins the ratio of geometric means, so it cannot catch the prose.

- **[severity: bug]** vignettes/short-record-flow.Rmd:310-312 — "Each missing year comes from the nearest gauge with a record that year" is false read geographically, which is how a reader will read it (the next bullet qualifies "nearest in place and size", this one does not). `wet_window_extend()` orders its chain in predictor space (easting, northing and log drainage area, each scaled). Distances from Maxan, from HYDAT 20260717 STATIONS: Goathorn Creek (08EE008) is 69.7 km away and supplies 1961–1971 and 2016. The Bulkley at Quick (08EE004) is 55.5 km away and has a record in all those years. Morice near Houston (08ED002, 86 km) is also in the chain. Write "the nearest gauge in place and size", as the second bullet does.

- **[severity: bug]** vignettes/short-record-flow.Rmd:193-200, 142; inst/cartography/wet_station.csv `short_record_map,donor` — The map caption ("the gauges its record is filled from"), the legend ("Gauge whose record fills Maxan's years") and "The gauges that fill its record are all within about 90 km" all put every non-Maxan gauge in the "fills the record" class. The article itself separates two routes ("Filling the record" against "Borrowing neighbours' departures"). Only 08EE004, 08EE008 and 08EE013 fill any year (`extension_donor`). 08EE003, 08EE020 and 08JA016 only lend departures to the second route. The "about 90 km" is Telkwa (08EE020, 90.2 km), which fills no year; the farthest gauge that fills a year is 69.7 km away. The guard at 142 (`extension_donor | carried_donor`) pins the merged class, not the sentence. Either name both routes in the legend, caption and sentence, or draw the two donor sets differently.

- **[severity: bug]** vignettes/short-record-flow.Rmd:343-344, 372-373 — "one with 100 mm more snow a third or more" is false at the Bulkley near Houston. Its one-predictor swe slope is 0.2837, which is exp(0.2837) − 1 = 32.8%, below a third. The guard `swe_gain > 32` is looser than the claim. Either tighten the guard to `>= 100/3` and reword (for example "about a third or more" or "a third or more at two of the three"), or quote the range.

- **[severity: fragile]** vignettes/short-record-flow.Rmd:93, 325-327 — "The borrowed departures give smaller drops, ... 3% lower in the migration window" needs the carried migration change to be negative. The guard checks only `abs(mg$carried_pct) < abs(mg$pct)`; it does not check `mg$carried_pct < 0`. The current value is −2.6%, which is close enough to zero that a rerun could flip the sign. `carried_text()` would then print "higher" inside a sentence about "smaller drops". Similarly, "at all three gauges" (344) and "On all three watersheds" (336) are `all()` over rows nobody counts, so a missing gauge passes vacuously. Add `mg$carried_pct < 0`, `nrow(fm) == 6` (three per model) and `nrow(sw) == 3`.
