## Mechanism

Each provenance or completeness sentence in the closing sections was written from what the author remembered about the pipeline, or carried over from the prose it replaced, and never read against the script that makes each number. The sentence names the best-known source ("flows from HYDAT", "the published product", "the build's check") and then states it for the whole population ("the numbers in this article", "every published value", "the layers"). Nobody enumerated the members: the inline-`r` numbers, the bundle fields, the columns the checker compares. R1 and R2 each fixed the instance they were shown, rewording one sentence. The same construction stayed in the sibling article. Also, a narrowing done in R1 ("every value was checked" moved onto the build) kept the universal quantifier, and the build does not support it. This is code-check.md's "Compression runs both ways": a sentence that gained a quantifier is unsupported until it has been executed against each source.

## Findings

1. **station-flow.Rmd:648-651, a false provenance claim (the R2 instance repeated in the sibling article).** "The numbers in this article come from flows cached from HYDAT release …, with provisional and real-time flows retrieved …, and from NGE's life-history table at commit …". The article's catchment areas do not come from either source. Figure 1's caption has 567 km² and 2,315 km², and "Buck Creek drains 24% of the area above the Bulkley gauge" (:267) is computed from them. Both are `catch$area_km2`, the area fwapg's `fwa_watershedatmeasure()` returns for each station's FWA snap (scripts/vignette_map_lib.R:24-41, bundled in station_map.rds and read at station-flow.Rmd:205-206 and 259-262). The next sentence credits the Freshwater Atlas only for the map's layers, so a reader is told those areas come from HYDAT or the life-history table. Fix: name what the numbers come from per source, for example "the catchment areas from the Freshwater Atlas", or drop "The numbers in this article". The pre-existing "These are the calls that produced the numbers" (:308) has the same scope gap, in a paragraph this branch edited.

2. **segment-discharge.Rmd:385, a universal quantifier the checker does not support (left inside R1's fix).** "The build's own check of every published value is its report." The report's own table heading is "every published flow, and four segment columns" (data/checks/discharge_publish_20260717.txt:27). The `watershed_monthly` parts publish runoff_mm, coverage and bc_fraction (scripts/discharge_publish.R:322), and the build compares only their q_m3s (:513-518, `col_select = c(..., "q_m3s")`). The `period` column is compared in no dataset. A reader of this sentence would take every column of every part as checked. Fix: say what the report says, for example "The build checks every published flow, and the segment parts' other columns, against what it was built from: its report", or point to the report without the quantifier. monthly-flow carries the same sentence (below).

3. **Low; not rendered.** The "concept DOI" wording in the citation-chunk comments (segment-discharge.Rmd:402, station-flow.Rmd:655, monthly-flow.Rmd:835) and in `wet_software_cite()`'s roxygen (R/wet_software_cite.R:23): "the concept DOI where its citation carries one". The helper prints the first DOI of any citation entry (:54, :69). For `sf` that is the Spatial Data Science book's DOI and for `tidyhydat` the JOSS paper's. Neither is a concept DOI. The rendered line does not use the word, and printing the DOI a package asks to be cited by is an accepted tradeoff, so only the comments and roxygen are false. Fix: "the first DOI its citation carries (a concept DOI for `wet`)".
