## Findings

- **[severity: bug — article states what its data do not support]** vignettes/segment-discharge.Rmd:153-155 — the new sentence says a segment where routed is under a fifth of the balance on its own line "is usually a reservoir or an outlet placed on the wrong segment". Nothing measures that across the 224,199 flipped segments. `research/water_balance_method.md:101-106` (the segment report's attribution) gives the Nechako Reservoir groups 26.7 % of all flips (placement, fwapg#24) and GUIC to PCIC's own runoff ("theirs"). Everything else is a flat ~5 % flip rate across edge types and stream orders (5.5 % at order 1, 91,874 first-order segments), with no attribution. The only evidence for "reservoir or misplaced outlet" is #58's 11 far-low gauges (`R/wet_source_pick.R:6-7` scopes it to "a main-flow segment ... more often than PCIC's runoff"), and even there several are unresolved. So the article attributes ~73 % of flips to a cause that is either unattributed or, for GUIC, attributed the other way. That breaks the repo rule (ours / theirs / unresolved), and no guard covers it. The old article (6be3e11:155) stated the rule with no cause. Fix: drop the clause, or limit it to what is measured (e.g. "as in the Nechako Reservoir, where the outlets are misplaced").

- **[severity: bug — article claim whose guard is a proxy that cannot fail]** vignettes/segment-discharge.Rmd:163-164 ("`r num(neither)` have neither, almost all of them first-order streams"), guarded at :143 by `sg[["no_lut_order1"]] > 0.9 * sg[["neither"]]`, with `no_lut_order1` from data-raw/segment_vignette_data.R:83 (`s.stream_order = 1`). FWA gives these edge types `stream_order = 1` whatever they are. Probe of the 369,217 no-lut segments by order and edge type: order 1 with 1400 (other flow / inferred connection) 207,963; 1450 (construction connection) 71,536; 1100 (side channel, secondary flow) 50,280; 1350 (construction line, *double-line river* secondary flow) 33,064; 1300 1,785; 1150 1,050. At least 84k are side channels and secondary-flow lines inside rivers, and every 1350 line lies in a double-line river by definition, yet each counts as a "first-order stream". The other ~280k are construction/connection lines, not streams. The research file says it correctly ("mostly edge types 1400/1450, also 50,280 side channels of type 1100", research/water_balance_method.md:98). The article reads as "the gaps are tiny headwater creeks", and the guard passes at 97.8 % because of the field's default, not the claim. Fix: say what they are (mostly connection lines and side channels with no watershed polygon), and guard on edge type rather than `stream_order`.

- **[severity: fragile — guard on a proxy]** vignettes/segment-discharge.Rmd:43 and tests/testthat/test-vignette_data.R:101-102. "The map's picks are the same build's" is checked as `identical(map$provenance$published$wet_commit, pub$wet_commit)` (plus the manifest URL, a constant). A republish within v1 at the same wet commit gets the same commit; it happens whenever the routed table (`WET_FWAPG_COMMIT`) or the segment table is rebuilt and the publish is rerun without a code change. `vd_published()`'s report regex also keys on that commit, so neither script tells two such builds apart. The map and values bundles could then come from different builds, and the guard and the Validation sentence ("the picks on its map are read from that build's published parts") would still pass. Both bundles already record `published$built` (2026-10-13T03:28:20Z in both), so compare `built` (and `segment_commit`, which the map does not record) as well.
